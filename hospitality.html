Hospitality

The floor is not an office. The people support should not pretend it is.

KSquad HR Partner is built for hospitality: guest-facing work, uneven weeks, and teams who cannot pause service for a workshop.

Shifts, not desks

Handovers, split days, and weekends are the job. A process that only works if everyone is in a meeting at 10am will not survive contact with service.

The guest is in the room

Tone, recovery, and judgement matter as much as the task list. Hiring and feedback have to notice that, not only speed and attendance.

Peaks and quiet

Season, weather, and a full book change what “enough people” means. Retention work has to be honest about intensity, not only about perks.

Owner and manager, often the same person

In a single site, or a small group, there is rarely a people department in the background. The support has to be usable by the person already running the week.

Situations

Typical reasons to start a conversation.

  • You are hiring the same role again, and you are not sure the process is the problem or the role is.
  • A good supervisor is struggling with people, not with service.
  • Standards slipped and you cannot tell whether it is staffing, leadership, or both.
  • You are opening, refurbishing, or adding a site and the people plan is still a list of job titles.
  • You want a few clear rules your team will actually follow on a busy Friday.

Turnover is common. It is not a personality of the industry.

Hospitality loses people for ordinary reasons: an unclear start, a rota that feels unfair, a manager who was never shown how to lead, or no one noticing a good person was ready to leave. Some of that can be changed without pretending the work is easy.

If you want to talk about your place specifically, that is what the first conversation is for.
